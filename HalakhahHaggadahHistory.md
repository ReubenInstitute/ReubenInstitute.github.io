# Halakhah, Haggadah, and History

This booklet series offers a clear and substantive exploration of Jewish tradition. Each volume focuses on a single holiday or concept, tracing its development from ancient scripture to modern practice. Written in accessible language, the booklets serve as both an educational resource and a guide for personal enrichment.

Each booklet meticulously traces the **"evolutionary tree"** of a practice by first grounding it in **foundational texts**, drawing on verbatim citations from the Torah, Mishnah, Talmud, and Zohar to reveal the ritual's original form. This initial layer is then enriched through **commentary and context** provided by major figures such as Rashi and Bartenura, alongside Targumim like Onkelos, adding vital historical depth. The process culminates in an exploration of **communal adaptation**, highlighting how diverse traditions — including Ashkenazi, Sephardic, Hasidic, and Litvish — have uniquely preserved or evolved these rituals throughout history.

**Appendices:** Select booklets conclude with a full-text appendix of relevant primary sources, such as the Book of Esther or the Idra Rabba. These are printed in a clear, space-efficient font to ensure the booklets remain portable while providing the complete sacred text for study.

**The "Annoying Kid"**: To maintain an engaging flow, select sections open with a **simple question or remark** from the **"Annoying Kid,"** a persona who voices the blunt, curious "gotcha" questions a reader might naturally have. This character serves as a humorous sidekick, flagging ritual inconsistencies — such as why **Modeh Ani** is often prioritized despite the Talmudic instruction to recite **Elohai Neshamah** — before the main text offers its resolution. By framing the initial observation through this persona, the booklet transitions seamlessly into historical and halakhic explanation, clarifying, for instance, that Modeh Ani's omission of God's name makes it uniquely suited for recitation before ritual handwashing.

## Booklets

The series comprises 24 booklets, two per month, covering key Jewish holidays, life-cycle events, and foundational concepts. Starred booklets (⭐) represent the annual observances — holidays, fast days, and Shabbat — while the remaining titles explore aspects of Jewish life and practice.

### January

![](@@/img/tu-beshvat.png)

[**1. Tu Be'Shevat:**](/booklets/tubshevat.en) ⭐ Traces the holiday from its origin as a tithe deadline in the Mishnah through its kabbalistic revival as a Seder of fruits and wine, with a survey of how the Seven Species have been read as symbols of the Land.


![](@@/img/the-jewish-calendar.png)

**2. The Jewish Calendar:** How the year is actually built: lunar months, the leap-year cycle, the rules for fixing Rosh Chodesh, and the centuries-long shift from sighting the new moon to computed tables — with a walk through the holidays as they fall.

### February

![](@@/img/purim.png)

[**3. Purim:**](/booklets/purim.en) ⭐ The Book of Esther and its laws, the growth of the public reading, the customs of costume, drink, and gifts to the poor — closing with a full-text appendix of the megillah.

![](@@/img/the-jewish-home.png)

**4. The Jewish Home:** Examines the home as a Jewish institution: hospitality, the Friday night table, the doorpost, family law, and the Talmudic sources that turned the house into a small sanctuary.

### March

![](@@/img/pesach.png)

[**5. Pesach**](/booklets/pesach.en) ⭐ From the brickyards of Egypt to the Seder tables of Gibraltar: the Exodus story, its laws, and the many ways Jews have told it.

![](@@/img/torah-and-mitzvot.png)

**6. Torah and Mitzvot:** The chain of transmission from Sinai to written and oral law, the nature of a commandment, the count of 613, and the medieval debates over what obligation actually means.

### April

![](@@/img/lag-baomer.png)

[**7. Lag Ba'Omer:**](/booklets/lagbaomer.en) ⭐ The counting of the Omer and the mourning customs around it, the traditions about Rabbi Akiva's students and Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, and how a day of semi-mourning became a bonfire pilgrimage.

![](@@/img/shabbat.png)

**8. Shabbat:** ⭐ The thirty-nine labors and their derivation from the Tabernacle, the laws of melachah and shevut, the Friday night and Saturday liturgy, and the adaptations communities have made to keep the day in modern life.

### May

![](@@/img/shavuot.png)

**9. Shavuot:** ⭐ The harvest festival and the Omer count that leads to it, the Sinai narrative, the all-night study, the Book of Ruth, and the debate over what was actually revealed.

![](@@/img/tefillah.png)

**10. Tefillah:** How the daily offering became prayer: the fixed times, the Amidah and its blessings, the structure of the service, and the halakhic arguments over intention.

### June

![](@@/img/the-fast-days.png)

**11. The Fast Days:** ⭐ The six fasts, their histories and laws, the question of who is exempt, and the readings that give each day its character — from Tisha B'Av to the minor fasts.

![](@@/img/bar-mitzvah.png)

**12. Bar Mitzvah:** The age of obligation and the sources for thirteen, the ceremony's growth from the Middle Ages, the aliyah and tefillin, and the modern celebration that followed.

### July

![](@@/img/mourning.png)

**13. Mourning (Aveluth):** The stages of mourning — aninut, shiva, shloshim, the year — their laws, the mourner's kaddish, and the sources behind the consolation.

![](@@/img/kashrut.png)

**14. Kashrut:** The permitted species, the separation of meat and dairy, the rules of slaughter and kashering, and the hechsher with its politics — plus the medieval and modern debates that shaped practice.

### August

![](@@/img/rosh-hashanah.png)

**15. Rosh Hashanah:** ⭐ The shofar and its blasts, the liturgy of judgment and remembrance, the Ten Days of Repentance, and the symbolic foods — tracing the day from biblical convocation to the modern service.

![](@@/img/kippur.png)

**16. Yom Kippur:** ⭐ The Temple rite of the scapegoat, the five afflictions, the liturgy from Kol Nidre to Neilah, and the day's transformation from priestly ritual to communal confession.

### September

![](@@/img/sukkot.png)

**17. Sukkot and Shemini Atzeret:** ⭐ The sukkah and its laws, the four species, the water libation, Hoshana Rabbah, and Shemini Atzeret's prayer for rain.

![](@@/img/simchat-torah.png)

**18. Simchat Torah:** ⭐ How the annual Torah cycle is read, the hakafot, the dancing with the scrolls, and the history of a holiday that developed late and largely on its own.

### October

![](@@/img/tefillin.png)

**19. Tefillin:** The four passages, the making of the boxes and straps, the laws of donning, the distinction between shel yad and shel rosh, and the tefillin found at Qumran.

**20. Mezuzah:** The passages inside, the laws of affixing, the case and its ornamentation, and the mezuzah's history from the doorposts of Egypt to the modern doorframe.

### November

![](@@/img/hanukka.png)

[**21. Hanukkah:**](/booklets/hanukkah.en) ⭐ The Maccabean revolt and the sources for the oil, the laws of the menorah, the liturgical additions, and the holiday's long rise from minor festival to major one.

![](@@/img/mikvah.png)

**22. Mikvah:** The laws of immersion, the construction of the pool and its rainwater, the uses for conversion and family purity, and the mikvah's history from the Temple to the modern community.

### December

![](@@/img/tzedakah.png)

**23. Tzedakah:** The laws of giving: the agricultural tithes, the poor laws of Leviticus and Deuteronomy, Maimonides' eight levels, and the communal institutions that grew from them.
