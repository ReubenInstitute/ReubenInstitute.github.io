# 20 Sep 2026 – DeepSeek 4.1, Claude (Code) Sonnet 5 – Studio psalm videos: word highlighting and batch export

*Studio, PsalmSeries, Media, Code*

The Psalms Android app already highlighted words as they were spoken, but the Studio psalm videos showed one static slide per verse. The first task was to make the videos highlight each word as it is spoken. Once that worked, the second was to turn the export into a script that can produce any psalm's two mp3s and two videos, individually or all together.

### Investigating the slide chain

The whole chain was read first: `PsalmVideo`, `PsalmVerseSlide`, `PsalmVersePlate`, `PoemVersePlate`, `Plate`, `Image` and `Asset`, plus `AudioBible` and `PsalmAudio`. My first proposal was an app-style highlight with rectangles, and it was corrected: only the word's colour changes, white by default and yellow for the spoken word, and nothing is copied from the app.

The timing rules and the working style were set at the same time:

* The verse duration comes from `cloned.csv`.
* The word timings from `alignment.csv` are a separate source.
* Highlight only at the aligned times, with no gap smoothing.
* No defensive checks; let failures show.

### The mapping question

Before any code, we checked whether the alignment's `word` column is an index into `verse.words`. It is: a 1-based index, in order. That matches plain enumeration for 2,416 of 2,527 verses. The other 111 verses contain maqaf compounds such as `אֲשֶׁר־שָׁר`, where the alignment counts each part as its own word but `verse.words` kept the compound as one token. The fix belongs in the model, not in the drawing code, and that was the decision.

### Implementing the highlight

* **`Scriptures/Bible.py`:** `Verse.words` now splits at the maqaf, with the maqaf staying on the first part.
* **`Studio/AudioBible.py`:** loads `audio/alignment.csv` and adds `word_timings(verse)`.
* **`Studio/Image.py`:** `draw_centered` takes a `highlight` word number and puts no space after a maqaf.
* **`Studio/Overlay.py`:** the plate text is white by default. Each verse exports a plain plate plus one plate per word (`.w{n}.png`) with that word in yellow.
* **`Studio/Video.py`:** each verse becomes a timed image sequence via ffmpeg's concat demuxer. Word times are offset by the 0.5 s fade.

Stills of psalm 7:1 showed the maqaf halves highlighting separately with no layout shift. Psalm 117 was rendered at 1920×1080, and the frames at words 1, 3, 6 and 8 matched `alignment.csv`, with the frame between words all white. The duration was unchanged at 17.8 s.

Only Scriptures and Studio changed, in commits `531d5db` (Scriptures, `master`) and `fa7a2f8` (Studio, `main`), both pushed with the token passed in the URL only, so it is stored in neither `.git/config`. The rendered `output/` folder in Studio was left untracked on purpose.

### How slow it is

Psalm 117 at 1080p took about 9 s for the plates and 155 s for the ffmpeg encode, on a machine with 1 vCPU and about 1 GB of RAM. Encoding is the bottleneck, about 94% of the time, and more CPUs would help, roughly 3× faster with 4 vCPUs. A plan with at least 2–4 GB of RAM would be the pick, and a DigitalOcean CPU/RAM-only resize is reversible. That discussion was postponed.

### From routes to a script

The exports were only reachable as Flask routes in `psalm-studio.py` and `studio.py`. `automate.py` already held a batch version, but it was stale, with `/sdcard` paths and the audio and cover steps commented out.

My first attempt was a separate `export_psalms.py` with a command-line interface. Testing it on psalm 117 exposed that audio export crashes without the square cover, because the mp3 tags embed it; the old script generated covers first, so that step went in. Then came the correction: the changes belong in `automate.py` itself, not a new script. `export_psalms.py` was deleted and `automate.py` updated:

* With no arguments, it exports four files for every psalm in `PSALM_NUMBERS`: Library mp3, Score mp3, horizontal Library video and vertical Score video.
* All the original number arrays and their comments are kept, commented out, with `PSALM_NUMBERS = [117]` active as the test.
* It skips existing files, and `--force` regenerates them (tested: all four psalm 117 files were rebuilt).
* The stale `/sdcard` paths were removed and the docstring updated with the current `PYTHONPATH`.

### The Media submodule

The Media repo turned out to be the `output/` folder, as pointed out. `ReubenInstitute/Media` has `psalms/`, `psalms+/` and `piyutim/`, matching what `Asset.py` writes. With 7.8 GB free and the clone needing about 465 MB, the local `output/` was moved aside and Media added as the submodule `Studio/output`. Comparing our test files with Media's showed only the horizontal psalm 117 video was new, and it was decided not to publish it. `.gitmodules`, the pointer and `automate.py` were committed as `ecc772c` and pushed, again with no token in `.git/config`.

`/root/output-test-backup` was deleted on request, and Studio ended clean, in sync with `origin/main`, with all six submodules at their recorded commits.

### What is still open

* **Alignment gaps:** nine verses still misalign (49:3, 49:11, 49:17, 55:16, 73:2, and a few more), and 55:16 will fail a render on a missing plate file.
* **Untested:** a multi-paragraph psalm, other psalms in a full render, and the Android `build_data.py` after the `Verse.words` change.
* **`automate.py`:** it has not run against the real Media contents. With `[117]` and no `--force`, it would generate only the horizontal Library video.
* **Speed:** the 1-vCPU droplet is slow for big batches, so a full run should go in chunks, exporting, committing and pushing in Media as it goes, and the server resize remains open.
