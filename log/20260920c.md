# 20 Sep 2026 – DeepSeek 4.1, Claude (Code) Sonnet 5 – Fonts: FrankCurledLamed fixes, the Fonts repo, Open Sans Hebrew RI, and the letters-by-niqqud test page

*Font, Studio, Documentation*

This was the day of the fonts. It began with a look inside `ReuvenSerif.ttf`, a derivative of Frank Curled Lamed, and with fixes to how its marks combine. It grew into a proper `Fonts` repo with licenses, our own RI variants of Open Sans Hebrew, a cleanup of all sixteen of those fonts, and a test page that shows every letter against every niqqud mark.

### Inside the font

FontForge was installed and `ReuvenSerif.ttf` opened: 150 glyphs, 2048 em, derived from Frank Curled Lamed. Its layout logic:

* GSUB `ccmp` merges two dagesh into `dageshhazak` and two sheva into `shevana`.
* GSUB `calt` turns `he`, `het` and `ayin` into `.wide` letters before sheva or dagesh.
* GPOS has mark-to-base positioning, meteg mark-to-mark and a vowel-shift lookup.

The font was saved as an sfdir folder, so each glyph is a readable text file. Every fix below was checked with HarfBuzz and PIL renders.

### Fixes to mark placement

* **Wide letters and meteg.** `shevana` and `dageshhazak` were added to the wide-letter rules, and `shevana` got a Meteg anchor. The wide-letter change made no difference in HarfBuzz (it already worked, because `calt` runs before `ccmp`), but it stays for other shapers. The meteg after `shevana` was a real bug.
* **Qubuts and qamats qatan with meteg.** Neither had a Meteg anchor, so the meteg drew on top of the vowel. The anchor and the vowel shift were added, as with the other vowels. Web sources confirm the standard meteg placement is left of the vowel.
* **Qof, vowel and meteg.** The meteg landed on the qof's leg. Per-vowel shifts and a small meteg pull-in were added for all 12 vowels, also when a dagesh is present, leaving about 40 units to the leg.
* **Final kaf, dagesh and vowel.** `finalkaf` had no Dagesh anchor, so the dagesh fell at the origin; one was added under the top bar. The vowel was pushed down by 115 units to leave the dagesh room, after the first attempt turned out cramped for the square dagesh. A list of 20 such words in Psalms came out of the local text (for example 139:8, 139:12, 139:15).

Two findings came along the way. HarfBuzz orders marks as base, dagesh, vowel, meteg, which made one of my earlier caveats wrong. And `calt` runs before `ccmp`, so the wide-letter rules see a plain sheva.

### Where the font comes from

The source is Yoram Gnat's `FrankCurledLamed.sfd` (2013, Culmus project, GPL v2 with a font exception), and the only copy is on the Wayback Machine. The Culmus source release has `Frank-Medium.sfd`, which has no curled lamed. Compared with upstream, our font dropped the Latin letters, the precomposed dagesh letters and Gnat's 24 GSUB and 11 GPOS lookups, and added the wide letters, `shevana`, `dageshhazak`, cantillation marks and our own rules. The Hebrew letter shapes are otherwise essentially the original.

### The repository

`github.com/ReubenInstitute/FrankCurledLamed` was created (public), and two mistakes were made and fixed along the way: first the wrong name, then our changes mixed into the "original" commit. The history now has the pristine upstream import and one commit adding the RI variant and the built font. The repo holds `FrankCurledLamed.sfdir` (untouched), `FrankCurledLamedRI.sfdir` (our variant), `FrankCurledLamedRI.ttf` (built) and `GNU-GPL`. In the RI variant the font names were updated, Gnat's copyright and the font exception were kept, and a modification notice was added.

It went into Studio as a submodule at `fonts/FrankCurledLamed`, pinned to `d3c1d2f`. `Overlay.py` and `styles.css` point to the new font, and the old `ReuvenSerif.ttf` was removed. Only the font-related changes were committed and pushed (`ccdd7c7`), built to exclude the other session's edits, which stay uncommitted in the working tree. `Studio/fonts/ReuvenSerif.sfdir` and `ReuvenSerif.backup-20260920.ttf` were removed too, so the font now lives only in the font repo and the Studio submodule.

### Open Sans Hebrew, and turning the repo into `Fonts`

The next thread was Open Sans Hebrew. The name table in `HebrewClock/OpenSansHebrew-Regular.ttf` credits Ascender Corporation and Yanek Iontef, with Google as the digitizer, under Apache 2.0, while the Condensed files in our projects have their copyright metadata stripped. The best official source is `google/fonts` (`apache/opensanshebrew` and `apache/opensanshebrewcondensed`), which says Yanek Iontef designed the Hebrew as an extension of Steve Matteson's Latin Open Sans. `jenskutilek/free-fonts` and `gsshab/OpenSansHebrew` are unofficial mirrors. All three were cloned into `/tmp/osh-sources`. free-fonts also has `.glyphs` design sources, and gsshab has no Condensed. The Condensed fonts in `Studio/fonts` differ by md5 from the google/fonts copies.

The plan for the repo was rejected several times before it was approved: scope it to the fonts repo only, and make the licenses per font, with no repo-wide GPL file. Then `ReubenInstitute/FrankCurledLamed` became `ReubenInstitute/Fonts` on GitHub, the local remote URL was updated, and the local folder was renamed `/root/WORK/Fonts` (the GitHub description was removed).

The original `FrankCurledLamed.ttf` came from the Wayback copy of the Culmus tarball, whose SHA-1 matches the Wayback index digest for the archived Culmus file. Culmus's own download link is now a 404. The repo got the 16 Open Sans Hebrew TTFs (Light, Regular, Bold, ExtraBold, each with italics, for both widths) plus an unedited `.sfdir` for each, made with FontForge. `GNU-GPL` became `LICENSE-FrankCurledLamed-GPL-2.0.txt` (the font's own notice says GPL v2 with an embedding exception), `LICENSE-OpenSansHebrew-Apache-2.0.txt` is new, and `README.md` says what the fonts are, that `RI` variants are ours, and the license per group.

### The RI restructure

The originals (`.ttf` plus unmodified `.sfdir`) moved into `orig/` with `git mv`, so history follows, leaving only our RI variants at the root. 16 RI copies were made, with `RI` at the end of the family name (`OpenSansHebrewCondensedRI-BoldItalic`), and the internal family, full and PostScript names and the name-table entries were updated so they install next to the originals. The `.ttf` files are generated with plain FontForge, and the `.sfdir` sources are the source of truth. The README documents `orig/`, the RI naming and the license rows, including the Apache-2.0 note that modified files must say they were changed. Three commits, `609ffee`, `5aa434e` and `0fbe637`, were pushed, with the token only in a one-time header.

### Analysing the fonts

The Hebrew coverage is identical in all 16 fonts: 27 letters, the vowels, dagesh, meteg, the shin and sin dots, qamats qatan, maqaf, geresh and gershayim, and the `FB2A`–`FB4B` presentation forms. Missing everywhere are the 30 cantillation marks, rafe, paseq, sof pasuq, upper and lower dots, masora circle, nun hafukha, the wide and alternate letters (`FB20`–`FB29`), and `FB1D`–`FB1F` and `FB4C`–`FB4F`. Latin punctuation is fully covered, except the soft hyphen in 5 italic fonts and a few rare characters everywhere.

Two decisions came out of it: the precomposed block is only a reference for diacritic placement and won't be developed, and since the wide letters are missing, any wide-he substitution for YHWH must stay disabled for these fonts, because that substitution exists only in code.

### Cleaning the fonts

* **Junk glyphs:** `.null`, `nonmarkingreturn`, `CR`, `NULL` and a stray `dotlessi.001` were removed. FontForge re-adds `.null` and `nonmarkingreturn` on TTF export and no flag prevents it, so that was accepted with no post-processing. An early attempt at stripping the extras corrupted the TTFs; a check caught it and everything was regenerated.
* **`.notdef`:** blank in all 16, as wide as that font's `space`.
* **Qamats qatan:** rebuilt as a copy of each font's qamats, with the leg extended to the qubuts's lowest point (−651 in Bold and ExtraBold, the segol depth elsewhere, italics extended along their slant).
* **Blank slots:** 37 per font (30 tropes, masora circle, rafe, dots, nun hafukha, paseq, sof pasuq), so browsers don't borrow glyphs from system fonts. Paseq and sof pasuq are blank placeholders that still need drawing.
* **Rafe:** in the 9 fonts that had a drawing under an unencoded name, it moved into `U+05BF` as a zero-width mark.
* **Glyph names:** Adobe Glyph List names without `hebrew` (`alef`, `sheva`, `etnahta`, `sofpasuq`), with `meteg` (not `siluq`), `holamhaser`, `qamatsqatan`, `atnahhafukh`, `lowerdot` and `nunhafukha`, and the `hb` suffixes removed from 25 presentation forms.
* **Filenames:** the 113 Hebrew glyph files start with `_`, so they group at the top in Midnight Commander and Android file browsers, and the Latin capitals have no underscore prefix. A FontForge GUI save would undo both.

### The letters-by-niqqud test page

The Open Siddur test page that was meant as a reference could not be found: the site blocks direct requests, and the pages found had no such grid. Two screenshots arrived instead, and the structure was worked out together: rows are letters, columns are the niqqud marks, one mark per cell.

The column order: bare letter, sheva, sheva na (doubled), dagesh, dagesh hazak (doubled), hiriq, tsere, segol, patah, qamats, qamats qatan, hataf segol, hataf patah, hataf qamats, qubuts, holam, holam haser. Ten meteg columns follow: meteg alone, and meteg with sheva, sheva na, hiriq, tsere, segol, patah, qamats, qamats qatan and qubuts. There is no dagesh with meteg. Empty cells: holam haser is filled on vav only, the final letters follow their own rules (ך: sheva, qamats, dagesh; ם: hiriq; ן: qamats; ף: sheva, dagesh; ץ: nothing), and meteg cells are empty on all finals. There are 27 letters, with shin and sin as separate rows.

The files are `test.html` (a static table generated by a throwaway script, now in `/tmp` and out of date), `test.css` (18 `@font-face` rules and one class per font) and `test.js` (a few lines: clicking a link sets the table's font class). The UI went through several rounds:

* The headings, the title and the doubled-marks toggle were dropped.
* Cell padding was cut to 1px by 2px and a mobile viewport tag added.
* The font menu went from a selector to buttons to plain 12px links in one vertical list, with gaps after the RI/original pair and after the non-condensed family.
* The page is normal LTR and only the table container is RTL. RI is listed first and is the default.
* The sheva na and dagesh hazak columns show only when the font class ends in `-ri`.

Serving it hit a snag: the nginx `/public/` location was serving everything as `application/octet-stream`, because its `types { apk }` block replaced the MIME table. `include /etc/nginx/mime.types;` was added and nginx reloaded, with a backup at `/tmp/nginx-default.orig`. The folder moved under `/var/www/html/public/` while we worked and back afterwards. The page was pushed as `6350b80`.

A later version widened it: three links (one per family) with an `(orig)` link each, every letter in all 8 weights in a row (Regular, Bold, Italic, Bold Italic, then Light, Light Italic, ExtraBold, ExtraBold Italic) with a weight label, and new columns for rafe (only ב ג ד כ פ ת), lower tropes, upper tropes, masora circle and the upper and lower dots, plus a nun hafukha row and a light and dark theme. A "blank font" trick for the page was rejected, so it shows the fonts as they are.

### Findings for the anchor work

* **Frank RI** has anchor classes `Bottom Narrow/Regular/Wide Point`, `Dagesh`, `Holam`, `ShinDot`, `SinDot` and `Meteg` (meteg attaches to other marks), plus an overline class that was dropped. Frank's tropes have no anchors.
* **Open Sans** has only two mark-to-base lookups (Hebrew and Latin), one shared bottom class for all lower marks, no mark-to-mark and no contextual rules. The anchor layout differs in 4 groups across the 16 fonts, and RI-ExtraBold numbers its classes differently.
* **Tanakh analysis** (Sefaria, about 1.2 million letters): vowel plus meteg occurs 31,182 times, a lower trope with a vowel 125,194 times, two same-level tropes only about 64 times (mostly in the Ten Commandments), and revia mugrash 260 times, 150 of them in Psalms. The Jerusalem lamed carries two vowels in 634 places.
* **Colliding today:** vowel plus meteg (overlap), and holam plus sin dot (only one dot shows).

The agreed plan, not started: split the bottom class into three following Frank's names, add `Trope Top` and `Trope Bottom`, use mark-to-mark anchors, write about five rule groups for the combinations, and work one weight at a time, starting with Regular.
