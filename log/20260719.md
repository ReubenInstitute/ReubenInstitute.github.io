# 18–19 Jul 2026 – DeepSeek 3 – Psalm series restarted, two‑week buffer built, Tikun Haklali integrated, export automated, pipeline self‑sustaining

*PsalmSeries, Studio, Media, Social, Code, Documentation*

The two‑month publishing silence was broken in the early hours of Saturday morning and turned into a fully scheduled, self‑sustaining pipeline by Sunday midday.  What began as a single upload to test the rebuilt Studio ended with 16 polished videos, a complete Tikun Haklali set, a revamped public catalogue, and a batch export script that automated the entire rendering chain.

### The restart

Psalm 84 was uploaded to TikTok at 4:30 am on 18 Jul.  Even in the dead hours, the channel showed life—views and likes arrived within minutes from the international audience.  The video was not pushed by the algorithm; it was pulled by an audience that had been waiting through two months of silence.

Psalm 93 followed on Saturday evening, scheduled for 23:10 Israel time.  By 3 am it had 535 views, 16.6 s average watch time out of 49 s, 75 likes, 7 shares, and a healthy 60 %‑followers / 40 %‑non‑followers split.  The non‑follower percentage was the growth engine: the algorithm was already pushing the content to new viewers.

By midday Sunday the numbers had climbed to 1 092 views, 129 likes, 16 shares, 10 favourites, and 3 new followers.  Average watch time rose to 18.6 s (38 % retention), and 16 % of viewers watched the entire video.  Demographics held steady: 50 % Israel, then Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Bolivia—the same international spread the channel has always had.  The two‑month gap had not killed the audience.  The Shmuelov voice still crossed language barriers.

### The buffer

With the pipeline proven, we shifted from single uploads to building a buffer that would eliminate week‑day publishing pressure entirely.  Over the course of the weekend we edited, diacritically verified, and exported styled text for every psalm from the schedule table through slot 23—16 psalms in total, including the full Tikun Haklali set requested for Meron.

Each psalm went through the full editorial pipeline: shva na, dagesh hazzak, qamats katan, and meteg on milel were verified against the text of other printed publications.  Real errors were caught and corrected—vattatziveni and sonneai in Psalm 41, a missing meteg on yachelu in Psalm 129, and the dagesh‑less fedut in Psalm 130 that was confirmed correct against the Masoretic text.  False positives from the assistant’s review were identified and dismissed.

English and Hebrew descriptions were written for every psalm that lacked them, both for the `psalms.md` metadata file and for the public release blocks on the website.  Missing Suno background tracks were matched to Psalms 16, 42, 77, 86, 105, 137, and 150, so every buffer psalm had a complete Score variant.

### The catalogue

`PsalmsSeries.md` was overhauled into a live, honest public record.  The schedule table was corrected to reflect the actual 18 May uploads (Psalm 47, not 44), a Date column was added with published dates, and the table was filled through 1 Aug.  The Tikun Haklali psalms were interleaved into the schedule with at least one day between any two, preserving the book‑cycling rhythm.  The only unavoidable adjacent pair (59 → 150 on 31 Jul–1 Aug) was pushed to the end of the active block.

Release blocks were added for every psalm: square cover image, English description paragraph, and a vertical video embed.  All video embeds were moved to a separate gallery section at the end of Releases—no headers, no dimensions, just a clean grid of players for anyone who wants to watch the collection end‑to‑end.  The Downloads table was extended with rows for every psalm, all pointing to the predictable GitHub raw URLs.

### The automation

Two scripts were written to remove the manual labour from future publishing cycles.

* **`batch_export.py`** — loops over a list of psalm numbers and generates covers (square, vertical, horizontal), audio (Library and Score), and two video variants—landscape Library for YouTube and vertical Score for TikTok/Shorts.  It uses the same classes as the Flask Studio and is re‑entrant: if a video file already exists, it is skipped, so the script can be stopped and resumed at any time.

* **`export_tikun_haklali.py`** — concatenates the ten Tikun Haklali psalms into four master files: bare audio (`TikunHaklali.mp3`), score audio (`TikunHaklali+.mp3`), landscape video without music, and vertical video with music.  The script solves the embedded‑cover‑image problem by stripping attached pictures to temporary clean copies before concatenation with the ffmpeg concat demuxer.  Audio files receive minimal ID3 tags: title `תיקון הכללי`, Institute artist and publisher, no track/album numbers, no cover image.  The script builds incrementally: while the batch export is still rendering, each run includes all completed psalms except the last one (which may still be writing), so it can be re‑run periodically until all ten are in.

### The method

The experience of this weekend crystallised a sustainable publishing rhythm.  All creative work—text editing, diacritic review, Suno matching, description writing—happens during the week at a calm pace, one psalm per day or so.  A single scheduling session on Saturday evening locks in the entire following week across all platforms.  The weekdays become completely free of publishing pressure; the social‑media manager can be at the beach.

The buffer is public the moment files are committed to the Media repository.  The schedule table in `PsalmsSeries.md` shows future dates, so anyone checking the website can see exactly what is coming and when.  Errors spotted in the buffer can be fixed before the scheduled publish time, with no retractions or corrections needed on the platforms.
