# 26 Sep 2026 – Reuben Institute – Language guideline refined, two log entries rewritten in place

*Documentation*

We noticed the language exemption in [Log.md](../Log.md) was being read too literally. It exists so a session's real emotional register — frustration, bluntness, a heated exchange — doesn't get sanded down into something polite and false. What it was never meant to permit is reproducing the actual vulgar words themselves, verbatim, in full capitals, as if quoting a transcript. Slang and rough language are fine; naming the specific profanity is not, and a couple of entries had drifted into doing exactly that, because the guideline was ambiguous, or in places missing outright. We rewrote the guideline as proper sentences instead of fragments, restated the pronoun rules (he or they for the assistant, it for a tool, never she), clarified where the passive voice belongs to the code, system, or hardware itself and where the active voice belongs to the work of deciding and doing, and moved the glossary to the end.

Two entries needed fixing under the tightened wording: the 21 Sep 2026 session, [Studio becomes MediaStudio](20260921b.md), and the 31 Mar 2026 session, [Blockquote formatting disaster, LLM limits confirmed](history-booklets/20260331.md). Both quoted the actual words directly, in capitals, in place of describing the problem underneath. What the original wording said doesn't matter now, and we're not repeating it here; that's the point of the fix.

Rather than adding a correction on top, we rebased the repository and rewrote the two original commits in place, replaying everything that came after them, so the language itself no longer exists anywhere in the history, not just superseded by a later patch. The MediaStudio entry also had our own decisions and actions sitting in passive voice, and one spot where the model narrating its own session had slipped into "I" instead of "we"; both are corrected now, and the four caps quotes describe the actual problem instead of the reaction to it. The booklet-disaster entry keeps its statistic exactly as it was, 189 instances, just naming the register instead of the word — serious, not casual, and not softened into something mild either.

One name stayed untouched on purpose: the `URL_FUCKUPS` dictionary in the code. That's dev jargon labeling real, unfixable defects in someone else's data, not an emotional outburst, and the guideline was never meant to reach it.
